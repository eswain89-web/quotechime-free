# Finite quote follow-up workflow

Use this four-step sequence after sending a quote or estimate. Replace every
bracketed field with a fact from the current written quote. Stop immediately if
the recipient declines or asks not to be contacted.

## 1. Confirm delivery

> Hi [First name], [Your name] from [Business] here. I sent quote [Reference]
> for [Work] on [Day]. Could you let me know it arrived? Happy to resend it.

Do not introduce a new price or deadline. The only job of this message is to
make sure the customer has the document.

## 2. Clarify one decision point

> Hi [First name], if you are still reviewing quote [Reference], is there one
> part of the scope, timing, or options you would like me to clarify?

If the customer changes the work, record the revision in writing rather than
leaving it in a message thread.

## 3. Ask for a decision

> Hi [First name], a quick check on quote [Reference]: would you like to go
> ahead, make a change, or leave it for now? Any availability I mention is
> current only and can be confirmed when you decide.

Only state real availability. Do not invent scarcity or imply a booking is held
unless the actual acceptance requirements have been completed.

## 4. Close the loop

> Hi [First name], I haven't heard back, so I'll close quote [Reference] for
> now and won't keep following up. If you revisit it later, get in touch and I
> can confirm the current scope, price, and availability. [Your name]

The stop is part of the workflow. An old quote may no longer reflect current
costs, scope assumptions, or capacity.

## Before each send

- Check the recipient, quote reference, scope, price, and date.
- Use only documented facts and genuine availability.
- Keep sensitive customer details out of shared trackers and AI tools.
- Do not promise a reply, acceptance, saving, or result.
- Stop after a decline, opt-out, or the final closeout.
